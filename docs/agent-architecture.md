# how Scotch thinks

Scotch separates looking from judging on purpose.

## 1. Scout swarm

Scouts are not asked whether an opportunity is good. They are asked to expand the possibility space.

- **Frontier Scout** searches broad role + ownership patterns with no industry restriction.
- **Adjacency Scout** explores one conceptual hop away rather than exact-match industries.
- **Venture Scout** looks for startup/scale-up signals: early product hires, equity, funding, 0-to-1, founder proximity, category creation.
- **Domain Neighbor Scout** explores companies that solve similar problem structures in different sectors.

Each scout returns candidates plus a discovery reason. It does not score them.

## 2. Evidence Collector

Fetches the source page and extracts role scope, ownership language, location, seniority, compensation, company clues, domain, requirements and source freshness.

## 3. Interpreter

Maps the role to the active lens: what would this person own, what evidence transfers, what is genuinely new, and what is merely keyword overlap?

## 4. Critic

The critic attacks the opportunity: Is this actually a step up? Is ownership real? Is the title inflated? Is the domain gap material? Would this narrow future optionality? Is company risk compensated by equity/scope/upside?

## 5. Scotch Ranker

Produces distinct dimensions: Ownership, Transfer, Step-up, Optionality, Venture upside and Risk.

`scotch_score` intentionally rewards a good move, not just a good match.

## 6. Opportunity Memory

Stores first seen / last seen, evidence snapshot, score history, user state and feedback. Future versions can learn from decisions without making the repository itself personal.
