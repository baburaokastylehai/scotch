# voice + design

scotch should feel related to the other things i build without looking like a reskin.

tripboard is a reference for taste, restraint, warmth, and product judgment — not a palette to copy.

## the feeling

quiet. tactile. considered.

closer to a well-made notebook, a printed label, or an object with some age to it than a recruiting dashboard.

the interface should make the market feel less noisy — not add another layer of noise.

## scotch, without the costume

let the name influence the product indirectly.

borrow qualities rather than clichés:

- depth rather than gloss
- warmth rather than brightness
- paper, ink, peat, oxidized metal, amber light
- small imperfections and asymmetry rather than sterile dashboard geometry
- editorial hierarchy rather than component-grid hierarchy
- something that feels selected and considered rather than generated in bulk

avoid bottles, barrels, tartan, whisky icons, faux-vintage pub styling, tasting-note jokes, or copy that tries to make every interaction a Scotch metaphor.

the name should create atmosphere, not become a theme park.

## visual language

current direction:

- parchment / warm paper rather than bright cream
- deep peat green-charcoal rather than inherited navy
- restrained amber / oxidized copper as the signal color
- moss and muted mineral tones for secondary information
- hairline borders and inset frames that feel printed or labelled
- occasional asymmetric corners and geometry
- generous whitespace
- playfair display for editorial moments
- dm sans for utility and reading
- subtle texture created through tone and layering, not noisy graphics
- one strong reading column before supporting metrics

scores should feel secondary to the judgment. never turn the screen into six gauges competing for attention.

## composition

prefer one considered object over a field of little widgets.

opportunities should read like short editorial dossiers:

1. what is it?
2. why did scotch bring it back?
3. what would you own?
4. what carries?
5. what should make you hesitate?
6. only then: the supporting numbers.

this is not a job board wearing better colors.

## voice

lowercase by default. short sentences. warm, quiet, confident.

say what something does instead of naming the internal system behind it.

prefer: go look, worth a look, why this, what carries, what you'd own, what to watch, opens doors, maybe, pursue, pass.

## product restraint

scotch does one thing:

> it goes looking and brings back career moves that are genuinely worth considering.

search, scoring, company research, memory, and multiple agents exist to support that one job. they are not separate products.

## personality

don't try to be clever. recognition is better than jokes.

- “good role. wrong kind of ownership.”
- “less obvious. more interesting.”
- “this one stretches you in the right direction.”
- “great company. probably not your job.”
- “you've done enough of this already.”

## decision filter

ask two questions:

1. does this feel considered, or merely polished?
2. could this be any SaaS dashboard with another logo?

if the answer to the second is yes, keep cutting and make the hierarchy more specific to the decision scotch is helping someone make.
