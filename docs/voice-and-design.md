# voice + design

scotch should feel related to the other things i build without looking like a reskin.

## the feeling

quiet. tactile. considered.

closer to a well-made notebook you keep coming back to than a recruiting dashboard.

the interface should make the market feel less noisy — not add another layer of noise.

## visual language

- warm cream background
- deep navy for structure and primary actions
- copper as the small signal that pulls your eye
- generous padding
- rounded cards
- very light borders and shadows
- playfair display for important headings
- dm sans everywhere else
- no neon
- no hard dashboard chrome
- no dense tables unless the task really needs one

scores should feel secondary to the judgment. never turn the screen into six gauges competing for attention.

## voice

lowercase by default. short sentences. warm, quiet, confident.

say what something does instead of naming the internal system behind it.

prefer: look around, worth a look, why this fits, what carries over, what you'd own, what to watch, opens doors, maybe, pursue, pass.

## product restraint

scotch does one thing:

> it goes looking and brings back career moves that are genuinely worth considering.

search, scoring, company research, memory, and multiple agents exist to support that one job. they are not separate products.

## personality

don't try to be clever. recognition is better than jokes.

- “good role. wrong kind of ownership.”
- “less obvious. more interesting.”
- “this one stretches you in the right direction.”
- “great company. probably not your job.”
- “you've done enough of this already.”
